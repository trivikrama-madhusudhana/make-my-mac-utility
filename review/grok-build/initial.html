Pocket Notes · comparison

Three quiet companions for the same job

Capture a short note, find it later, delete with a chance to undo. All three options use the same capabilities and fictional sample notes. Nothing here writes to disk.

Recommendation: Option 1 — Capture Column. Show focuses the note field, so a compact capture-first panel keeps typing immediately present without a wide split covering the desktop. Search and the list stay one glance below. Pick another option if you usually hunt more than you jot, or if you want one-line Return-to-save.

These frames are HTML stand-ins for a borderless accessory panel — not WidgetKit, and not proof of Spaces, covering, or the menu bar. Use the controls outside each frame for empty, failed-save, and undo states. A Choose button only prepares a summary for you to send; it does not approve a build.

Option 1 — Capture Column

336 × 528 · warm paper · composer on top

Benefit: the typing surface is the first thing you see; day grouping makes a short history scannable. Cost: taller than it is wide; fewer notes visible without scrolling.

Tab through controls · ⌘↩ saves (Ctrl↩ on Windows keyboards)

Option 2 — Quick Strip

560 × 372 · cool slate · one-line capture

Benefit: Return saves a one-liner; the list stays dense and wide enough to read a sentence. Cost / trade-off: a paragraph is cramped in a single line — Options 1 and 3 keep a multi-line field.

Return saves from the strip · Tab to search and Delete

Option 3 — Find Ledger

420 × 468 · soft sage · list first, composer docked

Benefit: timestamps lead, so finding by when you wrote it is faster; capture stays visible at the bottom. Cost: the focused field sits under the history, so capture is slightly less prominent than Option 1.

Tab to the docked field · ⌘↩ saves
